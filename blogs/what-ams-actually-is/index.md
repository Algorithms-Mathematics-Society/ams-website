AMS (Algorithms & Mathematics Society) is an Indian organization that runs
competitions in quantitative reasoning and systems engineering and builds
Access, a platform for coding assessments.

For someone who enjoys probability or mathematics, Derive is a place to test
that interest. For someone drawn to C++ and performance, Ascent offers a
different challenge. Teams can support the competitions or discuss using
Access for an assessment of their own.

Here is how those parts of AMS fit together.

## Derive: work through quantitative problems

Derive brings probability, market reasoning, and mathematical problem solving
into a timed competition. It gives participants a defined set of problems to
work through and a chance to compare their performance within the same round.

The 2026 edition progressed through two online rounds to an in-person final:

* **Round 1:** 2,500+ participants entered.
* **Round 2:** 150 participants advanced.
* **Finals:** 50 qualified, and 33 competed at IIT Bombay in July 2026.

Jane Street was the Apex Partner. QRT was the Convergence Partner for the
finals. Alongside the contest, finalists had opportunities to meet one another
and speak with people from partner firms.

Explore the [Derive edition overview](/derive) for the format and partners, or
see the [event photographs](/gallery).

## Ascent: work on correctness and performance

Ascent focuses on C++, algorithmic optimization, and performance engineering.
The central questions are practical: does an implementation behave correctly,
and how do its design choices affect performance under the given constraints?

If you enjoy understanding what a program does and finding ways to improve
it, the [Ascent overview](/ascent) is a useful starting point. It explains the
rounds and points to the official syllabus, eligibility rules, and edition
details.

## Access: prepare, run, and review a coding assessment

Access connects three parts of an assessment: organisers prepare a round,
candidates work in a desktop application, and reviewers inspect the results.

For an organising team, the starting point is the round they need to run:
the skills being assessed, the format, candidate device requirements, and how
results will enter their review process. Those details help establish whether
Access fits the task.

Results and session records provide information for review. Their meaning
depends on the problems, assessment conditions, and the team's criteria.
Hiring decisions also need the wider evidence relevant to the role.

If you are planning a round, [discuss your assessment requirements with
AMS](/access#assessments).

## The people behind AMS

Tilak Jain founded AMS and organizes its national competitions. Problem
setters, volunteers, engineers, and people working on partnerships and
community contribute to the contests and the platform.

As of July 2026, AMS reported community reach of 5,000+ students across
30+ institutions. Derive participants are included in that wider count. The
[team page](/team) introduces the people responsible for different parts of
the work and explains how to get in touch about contributing.

## Where to begin

If you want to compete, choose [Derive](/derive) or [Ascent](/ascent) based on
the work that interests you, then check the edition's requirements.

If you represent a firm, start with the skills and audience relevant to your
team. The [partnerships page](/access) explains the competitions, the assessment
offer, and how to discuss a scope with AMS.

For more context on the purpose behind the contests, read
[why AMS runs competitions](/blog/welcome-to-ams).
